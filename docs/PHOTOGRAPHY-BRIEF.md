# Navora Global — photography brief

**For:** commissioned photographer or graphic designer
**Client:** Navora Global Limited
**Deliverable:** 12 website images, plus product photography
**Replaces:** stock placeholders currently on the site

---

## 1. Who the client is, and what the pictures must say

Navora Global Limited is a **UK-registered food trading and market-access
company**. It connects farmers, producers and small food brands in India —
particularly Kerala — with importers, distributors, retailers and food-service
buyers internationally. It also sells one own-brand product, Navora Honey Shot.

The website exists to make business buyers trust the company enough to send an
enquiry. Every photograph has one job: **make Navora look like a real, working,
credible trading business.**

Categories currently handled: ginger, cardamom, honey, millets, rice, with
fresh produce planned.

### The tone to hit

Practical, warm, grounded, internationally minded. Think a good trade or
business-documentary commission: honest daylight, real hands, real produce,
nothing staged to perfection.

### The tone to avoid — this matters

The site this replaces was styled as a luxury wellness brand, and the client
has deliberately moved away from it. Please avoid:

- **Luxury/wellness styling** — dark moody backgrounds, gold accents, spa-like
  arrangements, honey dripping in slow-motion hero style
- **Travel-brochure India** — marigolds, incense, temples, colour-drenched
  markets shot for tourism. This is a food trading company, not a destination
- **Heavy grading** — no teal-and-orange, no crushed blacks, no fashionable
  filters. Colour should be true, because buyers judge produce by its colour
- **Posed stock clichés** — the handshake, the farmer beaming at camera holding
  up produce, the businessman pointing at a clipboard
- **Anything implying scale the client does not have.** No large factories, no
  fleets of lorries, no vast plantations. Navora is a growing business and the
  photography must not oversell it

### Brand palette, for reference

The site is warm off-white with deep forest green and a muted amber accent.
Images sit on a light background. Photographs with clean, bright, naturally-lit
tones sit well; very dark or heavily saturated images fight the page.

```
Paper   #FAF8F4      Green   #1B4332      Amber   #C98A25
```

## 2. The honesty rules — please read before shooting

This is unusual, and it is the client's firm instruction. Navora's content
guidelines state:

> Do not use generic stock images in a way that implies they show Navora's real
> farmers, facilities, offices or products.

In practice, for you:

1. **Do not photograph a facility, farm or team and let it imply it is
   Navora's** unless it genuinely is part of Navora's supplier network. If you
   shoot a generic farm, say so on delivery and it will be captioned
   "Illustrative image" on the site.
2. **Anything shown as a certification, test report or document must be real.**
   Please don't style a fake certificate, clipboard or lab scene as set
   dressing. If a shot needs paperwork in frame, it must be genuine or
   unreadable.
3. **No implied health or medicinal claims.** No pill-adjacent styling, no
   "remedy" arrangements, nothing that reads as medicine rather than food.
4. **Produce must look like itself.** No colour-boosting the turmeric, no
   glycerine on the ginger. Buyers assess grade and colour from these images.

Tell us which frames are genuine Navora/supplier locations and which are
generic. We label them differently on the site.

---

## 3. How the site crops — read before composing

Every image is displayed **centre-cropped to fill its frame** (CSS
`object-fit: cover`). The site never letterboxes. This has three consequences:

- **Shoot loose.** Leave generous margin on all four sides. Anything near the
  edge can be cropped away on some screen sizes.
- **Keep the subject central.** Off-centre compositions will be cut oddly.
- **Two aspect ratios only.** 4:3 for everything except five square tiles.
  Shoot 4:3 natively where you can, or leave enough room to crop to it.

Images display at roughly 500–700px wide on a desktop — moderate, not
full-bleed. **Busy wide scenes will not read at that size.** Favour one clear
subject per frame.

## 4. Shot list

Filenames are exact and must be used as given.

### Priority 1 — these should be genuine Navora or supplier locations

These three imply Navora's real operation, so authentic photography matters
most here.

---

**`sourcing-farm.webp`** · 4:3 · min 1600px wide
*Appears: About Us (Our story), Responsible Sourcing (page header)*

A smallholder farm or growing plot in India, ideally Kerala. Ginger, cardamom,
or a mixed smallholding. Should feel specific and real — this particular place,
not "a farm".

Best in soft morning or late-afternoon light. A person working is welcome but
should be occupied with the work, not posing. If the grower is identifiable, we
need a signed release.

*Avoid:* drone shots of vast estates. The client works with small and
medium-scale producers and must not imply otherwise.

---

**`sourcing-inspection.webp`** · 4:3 · min 1600px wide
*Appears: Responsible Sourcing, beside the accuracy statement*

Produce being graded, sorted or checked **by hand**. Close in — hands, product,
a sorting tray or scale. This image carries the page's central claim that
Navora checks what it sources, so it must look like genuine work in progress.

*Avoid:* laboratory styling, white coats, microscopes. The client makes no
laboratory claims and the image must not suggest otherwise.

---

**`about-partnership.webp`** · 4:3 · min 1400px wide
*Appears: Home page (Who we are), About Us (page header)*

Two people in genuine conversation over a product sample — a supplier or
producer with a buyer. The subject is the **relationship**, which is what the
company sells.

Shoot candidly. Both people engaged with the produce or with each other, not
with the camera. Neutral, uncluttered setting: a table, a workshop, a packing
area.

*Avoid:* the handshake. Releases needed for both people.

---

### Priority 2 — commissioned preferred, good stock acceptable

**`hero-sourcing.webp`** · 4:3 · **min 2000px wide** · *the most important image*
*Appears: Home page header — the first thing every visitor sees*

Must communicate *Indian food products heading for international buyers*.

Strongest direction: graded produce prepared for trade — filled sacks or crates
of cardamom, ginger or rice in a clean packing or storage space. Hands filling
or closing a sack works well. Enough depth to feel like a working environment,
one clear subject in focus.

*Avoid:* a pure landscape or field. That says farming; this image needs to say
trade. Also avoid an empty warehouse, which says logistics without product.

---

**`business-trade.webp`** · 4:3 · min 1400px wide
*Appears: For Business (page header)*

The export side: sealed cartons, a stacked pallet, sacks staged for despatch.
Clean and organised. Modest scale — a working consignment, not a distribution
centre.

*Avoid:* branded boxes from other companies. Plain or unbranded packaging.

---

**`contact-desk.webp`** · 4:3 · min 1400px wide
*Appears: Contact (page header)*

Calm, uncluttered desk or meeting table suggesting a conversation is about to
happen. Notebook, laptop, perhaps a produce sample in a dish. Natural light.

This is the most atmospheric and least literal of the set — quiet and
unremarkable is right. Good stock is perfectly acceptable here.

---

### Priority 2 — the category set (shoot as one consistent series)

Five square still lifes. **These appear in a single row on the home page, so
they must look like a matched set**: same light direction, same background
surface, same distance, same treatment. Shot separately on different days they
will look wrong together.

Suggested treatment: overhead or slight three-quarter angle, loose pile or
filled shallow bowl, on a warm neutral surface (unbleached linen, pale wood,
matte stone). Soft directional daylight. Generous negative space so the square
crop is safe.

| Filename | Subject | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `category-spices.webp` | Ginger and cardamom together | The two lead spices — show both |
| `category-honey.webp` | Honey | Jar or bowl. Avoid the dripping-dipper cliché |
| `category-millets.webp` | Millets, loose grain | Texture matters — shoot close |
| `category-rice.webp` | Rice, loose grain | Show grain length and colour honestly |
| `category-produce.webp` | Mixed vegetables and fruit | Planned category — keep it generic |

All: 1:1, min 900px square.

### Also needed

**`og-default.jpg`** · **1.91:1** · min 1200×630 · JPEG, not WebP
*Appears: link previews when the site is shared on LinkedIn, WhatsApp, email*

Usually a re-crop of the hero. Keep the centre clear — some platforms overlay

## 5. Product photography — Navora Honey Shot

Separate from the above, and handled through the content system rather than as
website files. Navora Honey Shot is a single-serve honey product.

Required:

| Shot | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Front, straight on, clean background | Primary product image |
| Back, label legible | Buyers must be able to read the label |
| Single unit, three-quarter angle | Shows form and scale |
| Multi-unit or retail/case pack | Shows the trade format |
| Lifestyle, in use | For marketing, not for specification |

Requirements: true colour, sharp label text, neutral or warm off-white
background, even lighting, no reflections obscuring the label. Buyers use these
to assess the actual product, so **do not retouch the packaging** beyond dust
removal.

A forthcoming honey candy will need the same set once it exists.

Deliver at 2000px on the longest edge, with cut-out (transparent background)
versions of the front and single-unit shots if you can.

## 6. Technical delivery

**Capture:** RAW. Native 4:3 preferred; otherwise leave crop room.

**Deliver two sets:**

1. **Masters** — full resolution, 16-bit TIFF or maximum-quality JPEG, sRGB,
   unsharpened, for archive and print
2. **Web files** — exactly as specified below

Web files:

| | |
|---|---|
| Format | WebP, quality ~80 (`og-default` as JPEG) |
| Colour | sRGB, embedded profile |
| Size | Target 200–400 KB each |
| Widths | 4:3 → 1600px (hero 2000px); 1:1 → 900px |
| Filenames | Exactly as listed — lowercase, hyphenated, no spaces |
| Sharpening | Light, for screen |

**No** watermarks, burned-in logos, borders, date stamps or signatures.

Please also supply a short caption list: for each file, where it was taken,
when, who is in it, and whether it is a genuine Navora/supplier location or a
generic shot. We need this to caption images accurately.

## 7. Permissions and licensing

Because the site makes claims about sourcing, provenance of the images matters
as much as their quality.

- **Model releases** for every identifiable person
- **Property or location permission** for every farm, facility or workplace,
  in writing
- **Supplier permission** before any image of their premises, produce or staff
  is used
- **Licence:** worldwide, perpetual, non-exclusive commercial licence to Navora
  Global Limited, covering website, social media, print, trade materials and
  PDF catalogues, including the right to crop and resize
- Please confirm no third-party trademarks, other companies' branded packaging,
  or identifiable private property appear without permission

## 8. If the budget is limited

Commission in this order:

1. `hero-sourcing.webp` — seen by every visitor, sets the entire impression
2. The five category tiles — they must be a consistent set, so they are all or
   nothing
3. `sourcing-farm.webp` and `sourcing-inspection.webp` — these two carry the
   sourcing claims and benefit most from being genuine
4. `about-partnership.webp`
5. `business-trade.webp`
6. `contact-desk.webp` — good stock is fine here

Product photography for Honey Shot should be treated as a separate, non-optional

## Questions

Anything unclear about a shot, or if a location turns out not to match the
description, please ask before shooting rather than improvising. The honesty
rules in section 2 matter more to this client than any individual image.
